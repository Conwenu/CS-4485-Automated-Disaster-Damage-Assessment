# xBD Joint Damage Scale: Damage Level Definitions

This document defines the four damage levels used in the xBD Joint Damage Scale for building damage assessment in disaster imagery.

## No Damage

A building classified as "no damage" shows no visible signs of structural compromise. There is no scorching, no surrounding debris, no broken windows, and no damaged roof. The building appears intact in both pre-disaster and post-disaster imagery.

In the xBD damage scale, "no damage" is the lowest severity tier. Buildings in this category did not sustain any visible impact from the disaster event.

## Minor Damage

A building classified as "minor damage" shows partial burning, broken windows, light roof damage, or visible debris around the structure. The building remains structurally sound and habitable. In the xBD damage scale, "minor damage" indicates the structure can likely be repaired with cosmetic or surface-level work.

Examples of minor damage include: shingles missing, gutter damage, broken windows, smoke or water damage to the interior, and small holes in the roof.

## Major Damage

A building classified as "major damage" shows significant structural compromise. This includes partial wall or roof collapse, large holes in the roof, partial burning, or extensive structural damage. The building is no longer habitable in its current state but is still standing.

In the xBD damage scale, "major damage" means the building requires extensive repairs and is unsafe for occupancy. Examples include partial collapse, failure or partial failure of structural elements, and extensive fire damage to a portion of the building.

## Destroyed

A building classified as "destroyed" shows complete collapse, total burning, or otherwise non-existent structure. The building has been reduced to rubble, ash, or a foundation slab with no salvageable structure remaining.

In the xBD damage scale, "destroyed" is the highest severity tier. Buildings in this category cannot be rehabilitated and must be demolished and rebuilt.
